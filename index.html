01 / UNDERSTANDING MENTAL HEALTH

What does mental health mean?

Mental health is about our emotional, psychological and social well-being. It affects how we think, feel, relate to others and handle everyday challenges.

♡

Emotional well-being

Recognising feelings, expressing them in healthy ways and learning how to cope when life gets difficult.

♧

Relationships

Feeling connected, respected and able to build supportive relationships with friends, family and others.

☼

Everyday coping

Managing ordinary pressures, adapting to change and knowing when it is time to ask someone for help.

2 in 5

Adolescents in a national Kenyan survey experienced a mental health problem in the previous 12 months.

02 / THE PROBLEM

When stigma makes
silence feel safer

Stigma happens when people are judged, labelled or treated unfairly because of mental health challenges. In a school setting, it can make a student afraid to speak honestly or seek help.

  • ✓

    Name-calling and teasing: classmates may use hurtful labels instead of showing understanding.

  • ✓

    Fear of being judged: a student may worry that friends or teachers will think they are weak.

  • ✓

    Myths and misinformation: mental health challenges may be misunderstood or explained unfairly.

  • ✓

    Pressure at school and home: exams, family responsibilities, or money worries can pile up and affect sleep, focus, and mood.

  • ✓

    Bullying and exclusion: repeated teasing, online harassment, or being left out can make someone feel unsafe and alone.

  • ✓

    Loneliness, grief, or change: moving schools, losing someone, or changes at home can be difficult to carry without support.

  • ✓

    Hard to find help: not knowing who to talk to, worrying about privacy, or having few nearby services may make support feel out of reach.

  • ✓

    Discrimination: unfair treatment because of a person's disability, background, or identity can add stress and make it harder to feel accepted.

“A student who is struggling does not need a label. They need to be heard, respected and supported.”

04 / PRACTICAL SUPPORT

How can we make school a safer place?

Listening without judgement and helping a friend reach a trusted adult makes a real difference.

STEP 01

Listen carefully

Give the person time to speak. Do not interrupt, mock or rush to judge.

STEP 02

Take them seriously

Avoid saying “just get over it.” Their feelings deserve respect.

STEP 03

Encourage support

Suggest speaking with a trusted parent, teacher, or counselor.

STEP 04

Keep checking in

A simple “How are you doing today?” reminds someone they are cared for.

05 / QUICK KNOWLEDGE CHECK

Myth or fact?

Tap a statement to see the explanation.

FACT

Mental health shapes how we handle stress and connect with others. Looking after your feelings is an important part of looking after yourself.

MYTH

Reaching out takes courage and self-awareness. Asking for help is one of the strongest steps a student can take.

MYTH

Young people can experience mental-health challenges too. Support is worthwhile at every age.

FACT

Our wellbeing can influence daily life in many ways. If changes are worrying you, a trusted adult or health professional can help you decide what support may be useful.

MYTH

You can start simply: “I haven't been feeling like myself. Can we talk?” A trusted person can help you take the next step.

FACT

Small routines can help, though they do not replace extra help when someone is struggling. It is okay to reach out as well.

MYTH

Listening without judgement is more helpful than dismissing someone's feelings. Encourage them to talk to a trusted adult or call Kenya's Child Helpline on 116.

FACT

A parent, teacher, school counselor, or health professional can listen and help you explore the right next step. If you need urgent support in Kenya, call 116.

FACT

Stress may show up as tense muscles, headaches, or trouble sleeping. These signs can have different causes, so talk to a trusted adult or health professional if they worry you or persist.

MYTH

You can reach out early, even if you are not sure what is wrong. A trusted adult can listen and help you decide what to do next.

FACT

If speaking feels hard, you could write a note or message to a trusted person, or ask someone to sit with you while you talk.

MYTH

Different approaches work for different people and situations. It is okay to try another small step and ask someone you trust for support.
