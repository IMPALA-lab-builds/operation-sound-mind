01 / UNDERSTANDING MENTAL HEALTH

What does mental health mean?

Mental health is about our emotional, psychological and social well-being. It affects how we think, feel, relate to others and handle everyday challenges.

🧠

Emotional well-being

Recognising feelings, expressing them in healthy ways and learning how to cope when life gets difficult.

🤝

Relationships

Feeling connected, respected and able to build supportive relationships with friends, family and others.

⛅

Everyday coping

Managing ordinary pressures, adapting to change and knowing when it is time to ask someone for help.

02 / THE PROBLEM

When stigma makes
silence feel safer

Stigma happens when people are judged, labelled or treated unfairly because of mental health challenges. In a school setting, it can make a student afraid to speak honestly or seek help.

Name-calling and teasing: classmates may use hurtful labels instead of showing understanding.

Fear of being judged: a student may worry that friends or teachers will think they are weak or attention-seeking.

Myths and misinformation: mental health difficulties may be misunderstood or explained in ways that discourage care.

Not knowing where to go: students may not know which trusted adult or service can help.

“A student who is struggling does not need a label. They need to be heard, respected and supported.”

03 / WHY STUDENTS MAY STAY QUIET

What can stop someone from asking for help?

Research involving Kenyan secondary school students has described avoidance, fear of stigma and uncertainty about where to find support.

🙈

“People might laugh at me.”

Fear of gossip, bullying or being treated differently can make opening up feel risky.

💡 Reframe perspective

True strength starts when we break silence. Trusted teachers, guidance counselors, or the 116 helpline protect your confidentiality.

🤐

“I should handle it alone.”

Some students feel pressure to hide their feelings or believe asking for help means they are weak.

💡 Reframe perspective

No one expects you to set a broken bone alone; mental health challenges are just as real and deserve qualified care.

🧭

“I don't know where to go.”

Even when support exists, students may not know who to approach or what will happen next.

💡 Reframe perspective

Kenya’s National Child Helpline 116 is free from any mobile network, 24/7. Trained counsellors guide you one safe step at a time.

04 / PRACTICAL SUPPORT

How can we make school a safer place?

You do not have to solve another person's problems. Listening without judgement and helping them reach a trusted adult can make a real difference.

STEP 01

Listen carefully

Give the person time to speak. Do not interrupt, mock or rush to judge.

STEP 02

Take them seriously

Avoid saying “just get over it.” Their feelings deserve respect.

STEP 03

Encourage support

Suggest speaking with a trusted parent, guardian, teacher or school counsellor.

STEP 04

Keep checking in

A simple “How are you doing today?” can remind someone they are not forgotten.

If a friend says they may hurt themselves or someone else, do not promise to keep it secret. Tell a trusted adult immediately and get urgent help.

05 / QUICK KNOWLEDGE CHECK

Myth or fact?

Test a few common ideas. Tap an answer to see the explanation.

FACT

Mental health shapes how our body functions, how well we study in class, and how we handle stress. Looking after your feelings is vital to living a wholesome, healthy life.

MYTH

Reaching out requires remarkable courage and self-awareness. Admitting that you are overwhelmed and seeking guidance is one of the bravest things a student can do.

FACT

Active listening without criticizing creates a safe refuge. Frequently, giving someone space to speak honestly helps relieve their burden and empowers them to seek trusted guidance.

MYTH

Mental illnesses are often invisible from the outside. A classmate might appear high-achieving, cheerful, and popular while enduring silent, severe distress on the inside.
